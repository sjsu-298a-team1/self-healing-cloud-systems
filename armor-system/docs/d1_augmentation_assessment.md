# D1 data augmentation assessment

Generated by `armor-system/data/pipeline.py` from `armor-system/data/artifacts/d1/augmentation/augmentation_assessment.json`; do not edit by hand.

**Decision: not adopted.** Do not adopt augmentation now. Keep the official benchmark and the data-preparation pipeline free of synthetic data. Revisit only as a labelled, train-only sensitivity experiment after a Model 1 baseline exists, limited to masking and jitter on live non-constant entries.

## Calculations behind the decision

- Median per node-channel spread (std) of live, non-constant series in the Model 1 training period: 1.000; jitter sigmas considered [0.01, 0.05, 0.1] are 1%, 5%, 10% of that spread.
- 80.5% of training entries are exactly zero; 230 entries belong to structurally absent blocks and 4,206 entries are constant, so jitter would invent signal there.
- Lag-1 autocorrelation of system-level series: median 0.64, 10th percentile 0.23 over 92 non-constant channels (temporal structure matters).
- Metric root-node mean |value|: 0.161 before injection vs 0.436 in the 5 minutes after (training cases): the fault signature is a short, large change.
- Smallest Model 1 training class has 4 cases ({'Container Hardware': 37, 'Container Network': 32, 'Node CPU': 10, 'Node Disk': 18, 'Node Memory': 4}); window-level augmentation cannot create new incidents, and synthetic incidents would be fabricated ground truth.
- ARMOR already ships its own augmentation settings in `config/D1.yaml`: {'noise_rate': 0.2, 'missing_loss_scale': 0.2, 'missing_prob': 1.0, 'aug_multiple': 3.0, 'node_mask_rate': 0.0}.

## Candidate methods

| Method | Advantage | Risk | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gaussian jitter on live, non-constant entries | cheap; mild regulariser for the 130-d numeric vectors | 80% of entries are exact zeros that carry meaning (series mean / absent block); adding noise to them invents signal. Incident deviations are brief spikes, so noise comparable to them can erase or fake a fault signature. | possible later, train-only, never on zero/absent entries |
| Time warping / window slicing | more window variety | features are one-minute aggregates with lag-1 autocorrelation median 0.64; warping changes the onset timing that defines the injection response, and windows crossing a split boundary would leak. | not recommended |
| Mixup / interpolation between samples | smooths decision regions | blends different fault classes or an incident with normal minutes into states that never occurred; labels become ill-defined (no incident durations exist). | not recommended |
| Synthetic incident generation (SMOTE-like, generative) | addresses the minority class | the rarest class has 4 training cases; synthetic incidents would be fabricated ground truth and are prohibited in the benchmark. | rejected |
| Modality masking / dropout | matches the missing-modality robustness goal and uses the real data unchanged | must keep structural absence separate from simulated absence (indicators); ARMOR already applies it in its own pretraining. | already part of ARMOR; evaluate in Model 1 only with indicators |
| Normal-period oversampling for pretraining | none needed: 3,714 normal samples exist | temporal leakage if it draws on samples after the training boundary. | not needed |

## If revisited later

- fit and draw only from the training period of an approved split
- never augment validation or test
- windows must not cross a split boundary
- keep structural-absence indicators fixed

No augmentation utility is implemented, nothing synthetic is added to the official benchmark, and the data-preparation pipeline does not alter any official ARMOR sample.
